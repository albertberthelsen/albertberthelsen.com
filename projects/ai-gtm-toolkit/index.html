Background

Kora Fashion is an early-stage B2B SaaS start-up founded by two former Meta employees. Daniel Wellington is one of its pilot customers. I joined as the first external hire.

What I do

Kora sells virtual try-on and visualisation that sits on the product page of online stores. Every customer has to run Shopify. I call leads together with a colleague on fixed days each week.

The problem

Finding the right companies was slow. Each one had to be checked for Shopify, and then we needed to find who makes the decisions and a number to call. By hand, that took around ten minutes per company.

What I built

Two tools. A lead engine in Python finds and qualifies the companies, and a sales platform is where the team does the calling.

How it works

The lead engine

It confirms that a store runs Shopify by looking for at least two independent signs. Then it finds the decision makers and their phone numbers through public company registers in six countries and contact services. It is connected to 10 APIs and runs overnight. One night it found 234 new paint and wallpaper brands around the world.

The sales platform

We log every call, meeting and LinkedIn contact here, against shared daily targets. Before each call it writes an AI opening, and afterwards it drafts the follow-up email. A LinkedIn pipeline tracks each contact from connection to message. New people on the sales team also learn the job on it.

Results

The pipeline now holds more than 1,400 qualified brands in 15 markets. We estimate the tools have saved over 200 hours of manual research.

I keep developing both tools as we go. The main thing I have learned is that most of sales is preparation, and preparation is something you can build.
